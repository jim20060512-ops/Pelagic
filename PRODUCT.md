# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

React + Vite

## Users

Primary users are divers who want to record the creatures they encounter. The initial focus is underwater photography enthusiasts, using a phone immediately after a dive.

## Product Purpose

The product turns post-dive photos into a personal, visual dive log: it identifies photographed marine life, captures the dive context, and preserves the experience as a shareable record. Success means a diver can easily revisit their sightings, diving footprint, and visual memories.

## Positioning

A social, visual-first dive log whose records are built from a diver's own underwater photographs and assisted species identification, then connected through following other divers and place-based sightings.

## Operating Context

The primary workflow happens on a mobile phone after a dive: choose photos, receive a species suggestion, confirm or correct it, add date, dive location/site, and depth, then publish the dive log. A world map shows locations where marine life has been photographed.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- A dive record includes photo(s), identified species name, dive date, location/dive site, and depth.
- The system suggests a species from a photo; the user can confirm or correct the result.
- Users can follow other divers and browse their dive logs and photos.
- A world map communicates what organisms have been photographed at each location.
- The intended product character is strongly social, highly visual, and gives recording a sense of ritual.
- Deployment target remains undecided.

## Evidence on Hand

No existing product code, imagery, species dataset, or brand assets are present in the project at capture time.

## Product Principles

1. Record the dive while the memory is fresh.
2. Let the photograph lead; metadata should support rather than interrupt it.
3. Treat AI identification as an editable suggestion, not an unquestionable fact.
4. Make a personal record feel connected to a living global community of divers.
5. Earn social value from real sightings and real dive logs.
